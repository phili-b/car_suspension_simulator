How it works

Spring: holds the car up

F = k · x. Stores energy as it compresses and gives it back. On its own, a mass on a spring bounces forever at its natural frequency f = √(k/m) / 2π. Cars sit around 1–1.5 Hz: lower feels floaty, higher feels harsh.

Damper: kills the bounce

F = c · v. Turns motion into heat by forcing oil through small holes. It only reacts to speed, not position, so it doesn't change ride height.

Damping ratio ζ

ζ = c / (2√(k·m)). ζ < 1: overshoots and oscillates. ζ = 1 (critical): fastest return without overshoot. ζ > 1: slow, sluggish. Road cars use ζ ≈ 0.2–0.4, race cars ≈ 0.5–0.8.

Two resonances

The body bounces at ~1–1.5 Hz on the soft spring. The light wheel "hops" at ~10–15 Hz between spring and stiff tire. Between the two the suspension isolates you best.

Comfort vs grip

More damping controls both peaks, but above the body resonance it pushes more road through to the body. Less damping is smoother there, but the body and wheel move a lot, and tire load swings, so grip suffers. Every setup is a compromise.

Compression vs rebound

Dampers are usually softer in compression (let the wheel ride up over a bump) and firmer in rebound (stop the spring throwing the body back up). Try a pothole with very high rebound: the wheel can't drop in fast enough.

Unsprung mass

A heavy wheel has more inertia: it follows bumps less well and loses grip sooner. That's why light wheels and inboard brakes help handling.

Pitch: dive and squat

Half-car model. Braking pushes the car forward at its centre of gravity, which sits above the axles, so the nose dips. The extra load on the front springs is the weight transfer ΔF = m·a·h / L: higher CG or shorter wheelbase means more of it.

Wheelbase delay

The rear wheel hits each bump L / speed after the front. Short bumps at the wrong speed make the front and rear push opposite ways, which rocks the car instead of lifting it.

Progressive spring

Gets stiffer the more it compresses: soft over small bumps, firm near the end of travel. The frequency plot uses the linear (small-motion) spring only.
